Concept

Porter

Buildings rent their amenity access to the neighbors who don’t live there.

Manhattan is full of two kinds of buildings: full-service buildings with doormen, package rooms, gyms, and pools — and walk-ups and townhouses with none of that. Porter is a marketplace between them: a building sells spare capacity in its amenities to non-residents nearby, and the building earns revenue from space it already staffs and maintains.

Packages first

The wedge is package receiving, because the pain is daily and obvious. A townhouse resident who works long shifts — the ER doctor is the example that started this — has nowhere safe for deliveries to land. With Porter, she pays the doorman building on the corner a monthly fee, and her packages are received, held safely by staff, and picked up whenever she gets home. No missed slips, no porch theft, no detour to a pickup locker.

Amenities come second: once the package relationship exists, the same membership can extend to the gym, the pool, and other underused building amenities.

The plan

  • Market: luxury buildings only — the service has to feel like a concierge offering, not a locker rental.
  • Pilot neighborhood: the Upper West Side, where doorman buildings and doorman-less townhouses sit on the same blocks.
  • Add-on: spare-key storage — a resident’s spare key held securely at a staffed building nearby, available around the clock.
  • Buildings get: a new revenue line from existing staff and space, with the building setting its own terms.

Status

Porter is a concept in development by Adrian Szczechura. The model, pricing, and building pitch are worked out; conversations with pilot buildings come next. This page describes the idea — there is nothing to sign up for yet.
